Frame.io is the standard for review in post-production. People look for a Frame.io alternative for three reasons: per-member pricing, a reviewer who is a client rather than an editor, or the need to deliver the finished work, not just review it. Here’s how the options compare, including where Positiva doesn’t fit.

Before you switch

Why people leave Frame.io (and why many stay).

Frame.io Pro costs $15 per member per month plus tax, with up to 5 members and 2 TB (plus 2 TB per extra member); Team is $25 per member per month. Annual billing saves 13%. These are the reasons that come up when people look for a Frame.io alternative:

  • You pay per member. Clients can review through share links without a seat, but everyone who works inside the project counts, and storage, projects and branded shares depend on the plan.
  • The interface assumes a post-production pro. A wedding couple or a brand’s marketing manager doesn’t always know what a range comment is.
  • Review isn’t delivery. Pro adds custom-branded shares, but there’s no final client gallery or portfolio behind them.
  • Editor support depends on your editor. Premiere Pro has a comment panel. Frame.io’s own V4 guide says DaVinci Resolve “will not be supported in Frame.io V4” (its help center documents importing comments into Resolve via EDL, in an article written for the legacy version), and creating V4 projects from Final Cut Pro’s share destination is not supported.
  • The free plan is small: 2 GB, up to 2 members and 2 projects.

And the reasons to stay: Camera to Cloud, range comments and on-frame annotations, the Premiere Pro panel, and storage that grows with each member. If you work in a team of editors inside Premiere, nothing here replaces it one to one.

The options

Each option, same questions.

Best for: photographers, videographers and production companies who review with the client and then deliver

Positiva

What it does well

  • Your client opens a link (no account, nothing to install) and comments on the exact video frame; comments export as markers: EDL for DaVinci Resolve, FCPXML for Final Cut Pro. Author and Studio plans
  • Comments on a point of a photo, and photo favorites that go to Lightroom Classic, on every plan.
  • No per-seat pricing: reviewers come in by link, one or fifteen.
  • Video versions keep their comments; once the cut is approved, the same tool delivers it in a branded gallery, with photos alongside, and feeds your portfolio website.
  • Files with folders, multi-file transfers and file requests without an account (up to 150 GB per file). Studio plan

Limitations

  • No native DaVinci Resolve or Premiere plugin. Markers are a file you import (EDL/FCPXML); nothing syncs live, and Premiere Pro has no dedicated export.
  • Single-frame comments with a pin on the image; no drawing tools, no range comments.
  • Review covers photos and browser-playable video (H.264, HEVC): no ProRes playback, no audio-only files, no PDFs.
  • No Camera to Cloud, no team roles or internal-only comments.
  • Storage is per plan (250 GB or 1 TB, plus add-ons), not per member. Video comments need a paid plan.
  • Our help center is in Spanish only for now.

Price: Free, €0 (15 GB). Author, €89/year excl. VAT (or €8.95/month), 250 GB. Studio, €219/year excl. VAT (or €22.95/month), 1 TB, with custom domain, multi-brand, proofing rounds and file requests. Extra storage: +100 GB for €19/year.

We wouldn’t pick it if your workflow is pure post-production, with several editors working inside the tool.

Best for: teams that review video and then publish it

Vimeo

What it does well

  • Review links with time-coded comments; guests comment without a Vimeo account (name and email).
  • A video host: the same file can be embedded on your site.
  • 2 TB of storage already on Starter.

Limitations

  • Priced per seat.
  • Free plans get one review link per video; more links and features need Standard or higher.
  • Their pricing page lists downloading review comments, but we couldn’t find an editor integration (Premiere, Resolve, Final Cut) in their documentation.
  • No photo delivery.

Price: Starter, $12 per seat/month (2 TB). Standard, $25 per seat/month (4 TB). Advanced, $75 per seat/month (7 TB). Their pricing page doesn’t break down annual vs monthly billing.

Best for: teams already on Dropbox who also review audio

Dropbox Replay

What it does well

  • Video and audio review, with lossless audio and version comparison.
  • Integrations with Premiere Pro, After Effects, Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve and Pro Tools; comments import as markers in Final Cut Pro and After Effects.
  • Works on files already in your Dropbox.

Limitations

  • Two subscriptions: the add-on plus a paid Dropbox plan.
  • Without the add-on, usage is limited to four or ten files, depending on the plan.
  • Their documentation doesn’t claim frame-accurate comments, except that Resolve comments “sync-to-frame”.
  • No photo galleries or client delivery.

Price: $10 per user/month billed annually ($120/year) or $12 billed monthly, plus a Dropbox Plus plan or higher.

Best for: small production teams that want editor integrations at a lower per-seat price

Krock.io

What it does well

  • Frame-accurate, time-stamped comments and drawing tools, per their comparison page.
  • Native integrations listed for Premiere Pro, After Effects, DaVinci Resolve Studio and Final Cut Pro.
  • Reviewers are free on every plan; images, PDFs and audio in the same project.

Limitations

  • Priced per user.
  • Production-planning features (storyboards, Gantt) you may not need.
  • No branded client gallery or portfolio on their pricing page.

Price: Free (2 GB, 1 user). Pro, $10 per user (from 2 TB; billing period not labeled). Unlimited, from $400/month for 100 users. Extra storage, 1 TB for $10/month.

Best for: agencies and brands with formal approval workflows

Filestage

What it does well

  • Video, images, documents, artwork, HTML and audio in one review tool.
  • Reviewer groups, due dates, version comparison, review reports.

Limitations

  • Built and priced for teams: Starter includes 10 seats.
  • No client delivery gallery or portfolio.
  • We couldn't find exports to video editors in their documentation.

Price: Free (2 GB). Starter, $199/month (10 seats, 1 TB). Business, $329/month (3 TB). Yearly plans get two months free.

Best for: unlimited reviewers on a small budget

Wipster

What it does well

  • Reviewers are unlimited on every plan.
  • Premiere Pro and After Effects integrations listed on their pricing page.

Limitations

  • Light is 50 GB and a single team.
  • DaVinci Resolve and Final Cut are not mentioned on their pricing page.
  • No photo delivery or portfolio.

Price: Light, $9.95/month billed annually ($11.95 monthly), 50 GB. Team, $19.95 per user/month billed annually ($25 monthly), from 250 GB.

Also worth a look

Two more for photo and video together.

Best for: editors who want client feedback inside DaVinci Resolve or Premiere, plus contracts

Exportlab

Plugins import feedback as timeline markers and upload new versions from Resolve and Premiere; EDL export for Resolve; contracts on every plan. Video storage is the catch: Creator, $14.99/month, 100 GB of photos + 10 GB of video with 30 min/month of HD transcoding; Ultimate, $29.99/month, 250 GB + 25 GB. Annual prices aren’t listed (up to 18% off). Where Positiva wins: video storage (250 GB shared on Author) and no transcoding quotas. Where it loses: Exportlab has the plugin.

Best for: creative teams reviewing photo and video with drawn notes

Picflow

Frame-accurate annotations, timestamped comments, reviewers without an account, Lightroom Classic integration. We couldn’t find an export to video editors. Plus, $15/month or $144/year (100 GB, Full HD video); Pro, $24/month or $216/year (1 TB, 4K). For photographers, see also our Pixieset alternative page.

How to decide

Match the tool to the reviewer.

If your workflow is post-production and you shoot to the cloud, Frame.io is still hard to replace. If you want the same idea with editor plugins and free reviewers at a lower price, look at Krock.io. If you also publish the video, Vimeo. If your files live in Dropbox and you review audio, Replay. If approvals involve legal and brand teams, Filestage.

And if the person giving feedback is your client, not your editor, and after approval you need to deliver the work with your branding (video and photos together), Positiva is built for that path. FAQ

The questions that matter.

Which Frame.io alternative lets clients comment on the exact frame?

Positiva, Krock.io and Picflow document frame-accurate comments, and Vimeo has time-coded comments on review links. In Positiva, comments export as markers for DaVinci Resolve (EDL) and Final Cut Pro (FCPXML). Dropbox Replay’s documentation only mentions frame sync for Resolve comments.

Which is cheapest if my client has to review?

It depends on the pricing model more than the list price. Frame.io, Vimeo, Dropbox Replay and Krock.io charge per member, seat or user, though most let outside reviewers comment through a link without a seat. In Positiva there are no seats at all: one account price, and reviewers come in by link whether your client is one person or a committee. For a solo videographer, Positiva Author (€89/year excl. VAT) is the lowest reference price in our table.

Does Positiva have a DaVinci Resolve plugin?

No. Positiva exports video comments as an EDL file that you import in Resolve as timeline markers, and as FCPXML for Final Cut Pro. If you want feedback to arrive inside Resolve or Premiere through a plugin, Krock.io and Exportlab offer native integrations.

Does Frame.io still work with DaVinci Resolve?

Frame.io’s V4 update guide says DaVinci Resolve will not be supported in V4. Their help center still documents exporting comments as an EDL to import into Resolve, in an article written for the legacy version.

Is there an alternative that also handles photos?

Positiva reviews and delivers photos and video in the same gallery. Frame.io supports images, and Picflow and Exportlab cover photo and video too. Vimeo and Dropbox Replay are video (and, for Replay, audio) only.

Can I use Frame.io for free?

Yes, with limits: the free plan is 2 GB, up to 2 members and 2 projects. If you pay for Adobe Creative Cloud, Frame.io’s Creative Cloud page lists 100 GB of storage, two users, five concurrent projects and unlimited reviewers at no extra cost. If price was your reason to leave and you already pay Adobe, check that first.
