Before you switch

Why people leave Dropbox Replay (and why many stay).

Dropbox Replay isn’t a plan, it’s an add-on: $120/user/year ($10/month billed annually; $12/month billed monthly) on top of a paid Dropbox plan, which is required. Without the add-on, Replay is limited to 4 or 10 files, depending on the plan. In exchange it offers something uncommon: lossless audio review alongside video, with files up to 150 GB and 12 hours long. These are the reasons that come up when people look for a Dropbox Replay alternative:

  • The real price is two subscriptions. The add-on’s $120 a year comes on top of your Dropbox plan: with Plus at $119.88/year ($9.99/month billed annually), one person pays over $240 a year.
  • Reviewing isn’t delivering. Once the cut is approved, the final delivery to the client (branded, with the photos alongside, with per-link permissions) is still a separate problem and, usually, a separate tool.
  • Photos are left out. Replay is video and audio; a wedding or a mixed campaign needs something more.
  • Frame accuracy depends on your editor. Dropbox documents that comments import as markers in Final Cut Pro and After Effects, and that comments from DaVinci Resolve sync to the frame; for everything else, it doesn’t claim frame-accurate anchoring.

And the reasons to stay: if you review audio (a mix, a voice-over, a score), Replay is the only tool in this comparison that covers it, in lossless quality. And if your archive already lives in Dropbox, not moving anything has real value.

Best for: videographers and production companies who review with the client and then deliver

Positiva

What it does well

  • Your client comments on the exact frame by link, with no account, and you take those comments into DaVinci Resolve (EDL) or Final Cut Pro (FCPXML) as markers. Author and Studio plans
  • One subscription: it doesn’t need any other plan underneath.
  • No cost per reviewer: anyone with the link can comment.
  • Photos and video in the same gallery, with favorites and comments on both.
  • Once the cut is approved, the same gallery handles the final branded delivery, with links that don’t expire, and feeds your portfolio website.
  • Files: folders for your material in any format, link transfers, and file requests so clients, collaborators or suppliers can upload without creating an account (up to 150 GB per file). Studio plan

Limitations

  • No audio-only review. There’s no equivalent to Replay’s lossless audio review.
  • No automatic transcription or subtitles, and no side-by-side version comparison.
  • It isn’t synced storage: Files (Studio plan) stores and sends any format, but it doesn’t replace Dropbox as your working drive.
  • Plays H.264 and HEVC video (no ProRes playback) and JPEG photos; storage is per plan (250 GB or 1 TB).
  • Video comments need a paid plan, and our help center is in Spanish only for now.

Price: Free, €0 (15 GB, 2 active galleries). Author: €89/year + VAT (or €8.95/month), 250 GB. Studio: €219/year + VAT (or €22.95/month), 1 TB, with proofing rounds and file requests.

We wouldn’t pick it if what you review is audio, or you need the tool to double as your synced drive.

Best for: post-production teams with several editors

Frame.io

What it does well

  • Frame-accurate comments, range comments and drawn annotations.
  • A comment panel inside Premiere Pro, plus CSV and text export.
  • Camera to Cloud, and storage that grows with each member (2 TB on Pro, +2 TB per extra member).
  • Handles photos too.

Limitations

  • Priced per member: Pro allows up to 5 members, Team up to 15.
  • No standalone audio review as a product of its own.
  • Frame.io’s V4 guide says DaVinci Resolve will not be supported in V4 (you can still import comments exported as EDL into Resolve).
  • No final delivery with a portfolio.

Price: Pro: $180/member/year ($15/month) + tax, 2 TB, up to 5 members. Team: $300/member/year ($25/month) + tax, 3 TB, up to 15. Free plan: 2 GB, or 100 GB if you subscribe to Creative Cloud.

The full picture, with more tools, is on our Frame.io alternative page.

Best for: people who review and also publish

Vimeo

What it does well

  • Time-coded comments; reviewers can comment without an account.
  • An embeddable player and showcases on paid plans; portfolio websites from Standard.
  • 2 TB of storage already on Starter, 4 TB on Standard.

Limitations

  • Priced per seat, and review links need Standard ($300/seat/year; $25/month) or higher.
  • You can download comments, but we couldn’t find an editor integration.
  • No photos and no audio.
  • Portfolios on a custom domain don’t support SSL.

Price: Starter: $144/seat/year ($12/month). Standard: $300/seat/year ($25/month). Advanced: $900/seat/year ($75/month). Their public table doesn’t break down monthly vs annual billing.

Best for: video and audio review on an archive that already lives in Dropbox

Dropbox Replay

What it does well

  • Reviews video and audio, with lossless audio; nothing else on this list does.
  • Transcription and captions, version comparison and due dates.
  • Password-protected links and digital watermarking.
  • Files up to 150 GB and 12 hours long; your material stays where it is.

Limitations

  • Requires a paid Dropbox plan underneath: the real bill is two subscriptions.
  • Without the add-on, a cap of 4 or 10 files, depending on your plan.
  • No photo galleries, no final delivery and no portfolio.

Price: $120/user/year ($10/month billed annually; $12/month billed monthly), plus the Dropbox plan: Plus from $119.88/year ($9.99/month billed annually); Standard $180/user/year ($15/user/month billed annually).

FAQ

The questions that matter.

How much does Dropbox Replay really cost?

The add-on is $120 per user per year ($10/month billed annually), but it requires a paid Dropbox plan underneath. With Dropbox Plus at $119.88/year ($9.99/month billed annually), the real cost for one person is over $240 a year.

Which alternative lets clients comment on the exact frame?

Positiva and Frame.io document it explicitly, and Frame.io adds range comments and drawn annotations. Vimeo works with time codes. In Replay’s official documentation, frame sync only appears for comments made from DaVinci Resolve.

Is there an alternative that reviews audio?

Not on this list. Replay is the only one that reviews audio, and in lossless quality. If audio is part of your workflow, that’s a solid reason to stay.

Can I use Dropbox Replay for free?

With limits: without the paid add-on, Replay is capped at 4 or 10 files, depending on your Dropbox plan. For ongoing use you need the add-on.

Which one also delivers the finished work?

Positiva: the same tool you review with handles the final delivery, with photos and videos in the gallery, per-link permissions and a public portfolio. Frame.io has custom-branded shares but no portfolio, and Replay and Vimeo don’t cover photo delivery.

Is it worth switching if I already pay for Dropbox storage?

If Dropbox is your working drive, don’t replace it: none of these tools is synced storage. The real question is whether you need the review add-on, or whether a tool that also delivers solves that part better.
